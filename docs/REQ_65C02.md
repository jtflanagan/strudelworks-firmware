# Requirements: the 65C02 card (soft core, L1, L2)

Status: **EXTRACTED 2026-09-18, branch `core_refactor`, from fw 2a5cbe1 / host 8fa0b0b**
(the ddr3_write_level tip, merged to master the same day). No RTL changed. Last IIe bitstream on
record: `dec_defer`, TIMING MET +0.046 (2026-09-14). The Au budget in §0 is an estimate until one
IIe build of this source is made on the Au part.

This document is the set of requirements a from-first-principles redesign of the soft core, L1
and the L2 page cache must meet **for the 65C02 card**: the card for the legacy Apple II models,
which must fit back on the Alchitry Au. Its sibling is `docs/REQ_65816.md` (the IIgs card). The
two are separate projects by the user's decision (2026-09-18); items common to both are stated
here in full and referenced from the sibling by section number, so they cannot drift.

Every item points at where the current design states or proves it. The pointers are into the
source as of 2a5cbe1; they are the evidence, not the specification. Items are tagged **[C]**
(common to both cards) or **[02]** (this card only). The ordering is: what is fixed, then what
the current solution does and why, then what it should not carry, then what must keep passing.

**Terminology.** The layer between the soft core and the L2 page cache is **L1**: L1-I (today
rtl/iline.sv + rtl/iscan.sv) and L1-D (today rtl/dline.sv). "Front", "fronts", "instruction
front", "data front" are retired (user, 2026-09-18); the old identifiers are quoted here only
where they are the thing pointed at, and are renamed as each module is touched.

(32 RAMB36, the same LUTs) is proven in the bench and was the named fallback (rtl/master_top.sv:79-83);
the IIe's 128 KB + ROM = 576 pages does not fit 512 pages, so the never-evict property (§4.2) is
a design question for this card, not a given.

**Requirement (HARD GATE FROM RUNG 1, user 2026-09-18):** TIMING MET on xc7a35tftg256-2 with
the full IIe feature set (the closed regime of §1.4, RamWorks 254 banks, WOZ floppy, HD,
Mockingboard/Phasor + SSI-263, mouse, printer, Uthernet II, NSC, VidHD signature, game port,
telemetry, the ring). Every rung of this card builds on the Au part as well as the Pt, and the
L1/L2 design is sized to ~45 RAMB36 and ~12k LUTs from the start. The Au baseline build is rung 0 of
docs/CORE_REFACTOR_65C02.md, done 2026-09-18 (the tables above).

**Requirement: scale L1 and L2 back significantly (user, 2026-09-18).** L1 because it is the LUT
count (9.6k LUTs and 9.4k flops for two 8x4 caches plus a retained set, three predictors, six
servers and their books); L2 because 64 RAMB36 for a page cache is more block RAM than the Au can
give it. The sizes are not to be inherited from the Pt design and tuned down; they are to be chosen
for this machine and this part. **The L1 and L2 decisions of the two cards are independent**:
neither project takes the other's cache design as a given, though each may learn from the other's
measurements (the miss-rate models of §3.5, the capacity finding on the IIgs) and share ideas.

## 1. System-level rules (all [C] unless marked)

**1.1 Figure of merit is bus cycles per second, not clock frequency** (docs/UCODE816.md:19-30).
The card runs at 100 MHz, fixed by the DDR3 controller (constr/pt_v2/board.xdc). Two clocks per
core cycle at a higher Fmax was costed and rejected: 157 MHz at 2 clk = 78 M/s, below target.
**Ceiling = 1 clock per core cycle = 98x on a 1 MHz bus.**

**1.2 Mode 0 is the old machine to the clock, by construction [02]** (rtl/accel_seq.sv:66-70,
647-651). cyc_en and din come from the bus driver; no card-side stall term may sit on that path
(the governor-stays-simple rule). Proven by accel_tb: no dummy, no fast op, every cyc_en the
driver's. (The 65816 card has no mode 0; on this card it is a hard invariant, because physical
floppies and timed loops depend on it.)

**1.3 No stolen cycles** (user, refined 2026-09-15). The card may pause the core whenever it is
NOT on the real bus (every miss does). It may never insert a pause between back-to-back real bus
cycles. RES' is the one place cycles may be taken (the ROM push, the checklist). Peripherals on
the card keep the core's time, one tick per op the core completes, never the bus's
(docs/ACCELERATION.md:110-135).

**1.4 What is slow is a matter of class alone** (docs/ACCELERATION.md:98-109, the user's rule).
Every $C000-$C0FF access, every $Cxxx write, $CFFF, and every $Cxxx page a slot answers is BUS
class and rides a real cycle. Everything the card's memory answers is fast. The one revision (the
closed regime, docs/CXXX.md): an access answered by THIS card's own peripherals (HD, floppy,
Mockingboard, the keyboard latch, the game port, the speaker) is served on the card.

**1.5 The governor is the one real-time authority and stays simple** (rtl/accel_seq.sv:324-368).
A leaky bucket in ops: every bus cycle adds the target (8.8 fixed point, ops per bus cycle), every
op fast or bus costs one, no op starts below one; the grant is a registered token that cannot be
withdrawn once given. Any "stall now" is a registered not-ready input on the consumer's side,
never a term in the governor. Every consumer (dispatcher, L1-I, L1-D) charges it identically.
The bucket holds max(4 x target, 1024) ops (2026-09-18: four cycles was too shallow).

**1.6 Leaving acceleration is a reset** (docs/ACCELERATION.md:399-421, fw 1cf5835). Once mode 2
has run the machine's own RAM is stale for good; there is no flush walk and none is required;
mode 0 and serving-off are allowed only while the machine is HELD.

**1.7 Every switch, bank and map change is visible to the host's mirror in program order**
(docs/SPARSE.md, docs/PROTOCOL.md record v6). A write the card serves must reach the stream as
an event, in order with the bus events; the host's AppleMemory mirror is the only way results are
verified. Full capture caps the target at 8x; sparse is the normal mode.

**1.8 Timing verdict = TIMING MET with zero failing endpoints, read from build/wns.sh, judged
over more than one build of the same source.** There is no magic slack number (build-to-build
spread on identical source is ~0.1 ns). A chain the census names by source register gets cut
even when timing is met. build/depth_baseline.txt names a NEW deep tail; the IIe baseline's own
tail outside UberDDR3 today: the L1-I CAM (rv_ic_q -> cnt_reval_drop, 15 levels), the L1-D serve
into the 65C02 flags (dq_sx -> f_z/f_n, 14), accel_mode -> filter counters (14), hd DMA -> L1-D
dq_v (14). On this card the verdict is taken on BOTH parts (§0).

**1.9 Verilator first; every rung explained before it runs; a bench failure is a finding**
(CLAUDE.md). The suite compositions that gate are in sim/suite.sh.

**1.10 A DDR3-free boot path [C] (user, 2026-09-18).** The card comes up, links, answers every
register and reports its DDR3 calibration state over USB3 WITHOUT the DDR3: a calibration failure
is diagnosed from the host, not from the FT2232H UART. The DDR3-backed TX ring (the buffer for the
one high-bandwidth source, the bus capture) is engaged by the host after calibration has been
read good and before BUS_ENABLE; nothing the startup checklist needs (START, LINK_STAT, MEM_STAT,
the DDR3 forensics, the ROM push's acks, a host-driven eye/phase rig if one is built) may depend
on the ring. **Today this is not so**: every card-to-host message, register replies included,
goes msg_arb -> tx_ring -> FT601 with no bypass and no fallback (docs/TX_RING.md:99-100,
docs/CLEAN_START.md:38), the ring's writer accepts nothing until calib_complete
(docs/TX_RING.md:383), so an uncalibrated card answers no register at all and the UART report
exists for exactly that case (CLAUDE.md; host pipeline.cpp:325-329 "a link that answers at all").
The requirement is the boot order: link and registers with no DDR3; calibration observed and, if
wanted, driven from the host; the ring engaged; then capture.
**Built 2026-09-18 (rung 2's first deliverable): the ring's boot lane and RING_CTRL, TX_RING.md
"The boot lane"; the host's checklist engages it.**
**Amended 2026-09-25 (user): the DDR3 is FENCED out of everything before full operation,**
docs/BOOT_LOADER.md. Three host-driven stages: a golden image at flash 0 that links, answers,
reloads the flash over USB3 and waits for the host forever; the user image's bootstrap (boot lane,
calibration read; a failure stops there and the user decides); then one OPERATE transition (ROM
push, ring engage, BUS_ENABLE, RES' release) after which all messaging may ride the ring. The ROM
push moves behind the calibration check (the host does it first today, pipeline.cpp:1715 vs :1827).
LINK_RESET becomes a full reset, sparing nothing: ICAPE2 IPROG back to the golden image, the DDR3
controller included (today it spares the controller, card_link.sv:668-677).

## 2. The soft core (cpu65c02, accel_seq, the bus driver)

### 2.1 The interface contract the board depends on

**2.1.1 One microinstruction = one bus cycle, one cyc_en pulse per cycle [C]** (rtl/cpu65c02.sv:45-55).
addr/rw/dout settle just after the edge that begins the cycle and hold across it; din is sampled
by the edge that ends it. There is no valid/ready pair anywhere: **back-pressure is expressed
only by withholding cyc_en.** cyc_en tied high = one bus cycle per clock.

**2.1.2 A cancelled clock consumes nothing, and the retry is byte-identical [C]** (sim/cpu_predict:
late cancel with garbage on din, retry identity). L1 asserts cyc_en speculatively and pulls it
low at the end of the clock (rtl/iline.sv:30-35). A redesign that consumes on a speculative edge
breaks both L1 sides. **This is the interface requirement most likely to be broken by a
redesign**; it constrains the microarchitecture, not the ISA. (A one-cycle core rewind was
considered and set aside on 2026-09-18: it moves the verdict out of the cyc_en cone but not the
byte out of the din cone, and every irreversible effect of a retire would have to wait for it.)

**2.1.3 Two data inputs, one per stream [C]** (i_din / d_din, muxed on the core's own cyc_is_i).
cyc_is_i = 1 for the opcode fetch and every cycle addressed from PC, dummies included; 0 for
data, stack, vectors and the LAST/FIXUP dummy re-reads.

**2.1.4 Register-direct exports for L1's serve cones [C]**:
- pc_addr = {pch,pcl} REGISTER-DIRECT, equal to addr on every PC-sourced cycle by construction
  (the addr mux's default arm IS these registers, rtl/cpu65c02.sv:350-353).
- p_flags REGISTER-DIRECT, final from the edge that begins the branch's opcode fetch, so L1-I
  resolves branches a cycle early (rtl/cpu65c02.sv:162).
- i_pred = next_pc, the next program-stream fetch address a cycle ahead; not a guess.
- d_pred / d_pred_v / d_pred_rw: the next data cycle's address and direction a cycle ahead, BY
  CONSTRUCTION (the next microinstruction's addr_sel is in this cycle's ROM read and every source
  it can pick is a register whose next state this cycle computes; rtl/cpu65c02.sv:148-158). The
  one exception is a next-cycle live-AGU address, which only $5C NOP drives. d_pred inherits din's
  timing: **the consumer registers it, always.** Contract-checked over both Klaus suites: 53.6 M
  data cycles, zero wrong claims.
- The opcode is taken from i_din directly, never through the shared din mux (rtl/cpu65c02.sv:601-610):
  otherwise L1-D's served byte reaches the ucode ROM address and the D-side prediction, a false
  path static timing charges at zero margin (three builds failed on it).

**2.1.5 Interrupts [C]**: irq_n level, nmi_n edge-latched; taken only at an instruction
boundary; no ABORT, MLB, RDY, SO. B is a property of the push, not a register. Entry masks I and
clears D (the CMOS behaviour).

**2.1.6 Reset [02]**: the 65C02 runs BRK's microcode with the stack cycles as reads, 7 cycles,
S -= 3, entering at ucyc 7 because of the ROM pipeline (rtl/cpu65c02.sv:804-834,
docs/DERISK.md:1288-1307). **At the system level the reset sequence must ride the real bus**
(three stack reads + $FFFC/$FFFD): the IIe's MMU infers a reset from that sequence, not from RES'.
Serving those seven cycles from the card left INTCXROM set from the last program and the slot
scan reading the ROM's self-test; found on hardware (rtl/accel_seq.sv:85-97).

### 2.2 The sequencer's contract

**2.2.1 Four op kinds [C]** (docs/ACCELERATION.md:70-88): fast (served on the card, on the
unified stream, no driver op), bus (driven at T_PRESENT), override (boot hold, HD DMA, VBL
lock), dummy (a parked $0000 read when nothing is offered; not on the unified stream; keeps the
video, the cycle-counting peripherals and the VBL lock alive; in the closed regime also the
keyboard-latch sampling instrument, docs/ACCELERATION.md:287-330).

**2.2.2 needs_bus = mode==0 || (class==BUS && !internalized) || (write && mode==1) ||
!serving || reset_seq [C]** (rtl/accel_seq.sv:397-398). fast_ok = !stall && image_ready &&
!invalidating && !op_inflight && !busy && !ovr_en. Nothing else sends an op to the bus; a fast
read the cache decided without a byte is decided AGAIN, never sent to the bus (mode 2 RAM is
stale).

**2.2.3 The decision is made from registered copies, one clock after the op appears [C]**
(rtl/accel_seq.sv:18-24). Cost today: settle + decide + present + the page cache's three + take =
**~7-9 clocks per dispatcher op, measured 7.7**. This is the number the redesign exists to
remove: at 58x on the IIe the dispatcher's 7.6% of ops cost 36% of all clocks
(docs/ACCELERATION.md:348-353).

**2.2.4 One op at a time, serialised [C]** (docs/ACCELERATION.md "Decisions"). The page cache
holds one op with no tag bypass; the sequencer presents the next only when the last is decided.
The core's seriality is what SMC coherence rests on (rtl/iline.sv:52-58): a write snoops the
lines and the fill in flight the same clock, and the refetch is behind it by construction. **A
redesign with any write-behind or out-of-order retire must re-prove SMC coherence.**

**2.2.5 The bus driver's timing facts [C]** (rtl/apple_bus_write.sv:18-84): T_PRESENT=2 after
phi0 falls, T_EMIT=2 / T_DIN=44 / T_CYCEN=45 after it rises; write data must be up early (6740 of
6855 write-readback pairs failed with a late tap); the address is latched at a fixed tap, one
value per bus cycle; the address must beat the reader's own snap (~17 clocks of margin); cyc_en
at rise+47 and the driver's sample at rise+52 leaves **~4-5 clocks**, so core_valid is cleared
on cyc_done and set only by a decision. Two consecutive bus ops ride consecutive cycles ("stay
merged"); the bench sweeps the margin down to 3 clocks.

**2.2.6 Internalized $C0xx ops [C]** ride S_FAST and retire with a SYNTHETIC COMMIT on the
unified stream so every commit-gated consumer (the MMU shadow's switches) sees them; quiet polls
drop their event word (rtl/accel_seq.sv:182-217, 550-558). A map-moving switch write holds L1 for
five clocks; which addresses hold is enumerated ($C00x writes, $C05x, $C08x); holding on keyboard
reads too cost the ProDOS boot 12% (rtl/accel_seq.sv:433-480). INTCXROM/SLOTC3ROM writes are
never internalized (the motherboard must see them).

**2.2.7 Overrides [C]**: honoured at the next decision; the core sits still; released only when
!ovr_en && !op_inflight && !busy && !d_pending; boot_hold's engage is combinational on the live
SYNC and watches the unified stream (a SYNC fetched from the card still counts).

**2.2.8 Posted writes drain first [C]**: nothing leaves S_DECIDE while d_pending (rtl/accel_seq.sv:667).

**2.2.9 Virtual time [02]**: vbeam steps once per CORE cycle (fast, turbo, L1-D, or a real
non-dummy non-override bus tick; docs/VIRTUAL_TIME.md). A stall (a floppy fill) holds a granted
credit without ticking: the program's virtual time stands still.

**2.2.10 Registers [C]**: ACCEL_CTRL [1:0] mode, [23:8] target, [27] game_dis, [28] swint_dis,
[29] L1 bypass (today fronts_dis), [31] clear counters; ACCEL_STAT; the seven-bucket fast census
(I/DRAM/DLC/DROM/DCX/WR/KBD) summing to ACCEL_FAST, the counters two flops from any cone
(rtl/fast_census.sv:6-15); the clock histogram (rtl/master_top.sv "WHERE THE CLOCKS GO", twelve
buckets that sum to CLOCKS). Telemetry (rtl/telem_map.vh) carries them and the host's tools
depend on the names; a change is a layout bump in both repos.

### 2.3 Functional invariants: what the oracles pin

**2.3.1 Exact cycle count for all 256 opcodes [02]**, including the conditional cases (indexed
page crossings, write forms always paying the fixup, ASL/LSR/ROL/ROR abs,X 6-or-7 against
INC/DEC abs,X flat 7, branches, the CMOS decimal extra cycle). Held as a flat literal table in
sim/gen_cycle_table.py, deliberately not derived from the core.

**2.3.2 The exact address on every cycle, dummies included [02]** (docs/DERISK.md:1096-1153): the
CMOS part re-reads its LAST address during an internal cycle (UC_ADDR_LAST); the abs,X/abs,Y fixup
depends on the DATA (carried: the last program byte; not: the effective address; UC_ADDR_FIXUP
off the saved carry); (zp),Y re-reads its pointer either way. 77 sites, 24.7 M cycles against a
real part. Unverified: the decimal cycle's address and reset's two PC reads.

**2.3.3 Interrupt entry holds PC across the two incrementing cycles [C]** (int_pc_hold). Learned
on hardware (a Mockingboard timer IRQ returned +2). No suite drives irq_n: cpu_irq_tb carries it.

**2.3.4 Decimal [02]**: the CMOS extra cycle is used to do the arithmetic (dec_defer from the
latched operand); SBC takes C and V from the binary result and N/Z from the corrected one. Bruce
Clark's decimal test is in the tree (third_party/6502_tests) and run by no script; Klaus ignores
N/V/Z after decimal.

**2.3.5 The two Klaus suites pass at EXACTLY 96,561,331 and 66,874,616 cycles [02]**; the
exactness is the regression check (a known delta, e.g. reset +2, proves nothing else moved). The
65C02 suite is re-assembled for the base part so $x7/$xF are NOPs (stronger than skipping).

**2.3.6 Carry gift, design-wide law [C]**: the high byte NEVER consumes a live carry; a dummy
cycle PROBES the carry and the increment consumes it saved (sim/gen_microcode.py:46-61). Elevated
in docs/DLINE.md:279-281: no serve cone chains two arithmetic events.

**2.3.7 No global conditional-load enable [C]** (rtl/cpu65816.sv:995-1009, learned on the 816: a
ld_if field ANDed into ~200 CE pins put all twenty worst paths on a CE; deleting it was +6.5 MHz).
Gate the one register that needs it. The 65C02 still carries a dead ld_if bit, folded away only
because its ROM is distributed (§2.5).

### 2.5 Accumulated complexity not to carry [02]

- Dead-but-live logic for a WDC variant that does not exist: RMB/SMB, ZPBIT, WAI wake,
  STP/halted; retired encodings left as holes (LO_B DIN, HI_C LIVE); ld_if set in 0 of 2048
  words and still gating every load (rtl/cpu65c02.sv:219, 630, 648).
- int_pc_hold's three ad-hoc terms with reset carved out "to keep that path bit-for-bit as
  measured".
- UC_ADDR_FIXUP: one microcode encoding whose meaning depends on the data, because one addressing
  mode behaves one way and (zp),Y the other for no known reason (the trace is the authority).
- accel_seq: swi_sq (a 4-then-5 deep settle shift register with a five-paragraph comment),
  sl_win/sl_claim_fast (two modules cooperating through one bit), dq_bank held not sampled,

## 3. L1 (L1-I = iline + iscan, L1-D = dline)

Both L1 sides sit in series on the core's cycle enable and byte (iline.cyc_en -> dline ->
cpu.cyc_en; rtl/master_top.sv:1293-1369). Neither has a request port: each watches the core's
live cycle and its register-direct exports.

### 3.1 What L1 must deliver

**3.1.1 An instruction fetch into any held line retires in ONE clock, and the byte is a
register [C]** (rtl/iline.sv:735-736). L1-I runs its own +1 predictor, looks up three candidates
in parallel from registers (step, recovery, hold; rtl/iline.sv:355-363), and the late verdict
only PICKS. One byte per core cycle; a mispredict costs one stall beat; a slow fetch still steps
the predictor so the next fetch is back at cadence.

**3.1.2 A resolved branch, JMP or JSR into a resident line retires with ZERO stall [02 today, C
as a target]** (tgt_serve, rtl/iline.sv:467; 47,588 zero-stall serves in the bench). Resolved at
the operand cycle from registered truth only (opcode latched at its fetch, the offset being
served, p_flags). "The conclusion cannot be wrong, only useless": the candidate serves only when
pc_addr equals it.

**3.1.3 A loop held whole in the two slots runs with no refetch at all [C]** (the pinned pair,
rtl/iline.sv:76-80, 908-912), and a fetch into ANY line of the retained set runs at the cadence
of one.

**3.1.4 A data read into a held line retires in ONE clock [02]** (rtl/dline.sv:942). Six one-hot
servers today: the zp/stack shadow (physical main pages 0/1, LUTRAM), the 8x4 set cache (virtual
9-bit tag), the last byte (serves every CMOS LAST/FIXUP dummy with no storage), the last row, the
keyboard ($C000 from the latch, the retire pulses kbd_rd so auto-repeat counts once), and the
on-card I/O reads. The byte to the core is one flat one-hot OR (rtl/dline.sv:1044-1047).

**3.1.5 A mode-2 write below $C000 in RamWorks bank 0 retires in ONE clock with NO classify cone
in the retire [02]** (w_serv, rtl/dline.sv:921-923: below $C000 is RAM class under every map). It
is spliced into every holder one clock later and posted to the page cache through a FIFO (4
deep). A write that cannot retire WAITS, never misses. LC writes ($D000+) still ride the
dispatcher: a known 9%-of-clocks item on Nox (docs/CXXX.md:850-855).

**3.1.6 Every retire is charged to the governor and ticked to the peripherals' core time [C]**
(turbo_commit / d_commit consume the registered grant; tick_q feeds vbeam, the floppy and the
Mockingboard). A retire without a stream word is a core cycle the host cannot place: instruction
retires wait for ev_room, write retires for ev_room_w (never dropped), read events are best-effort.

**3.1.7 The retired fetch never reaches the unified op stream [C]**, so nothing that moves a
switch or a latch may be served: all of $C000-$C0FF, the $CFF0 line always, page $C3 while
INTC8ROM is clear (the access moves the latch; rtl/iline.sv:41-50, 469-496), every slot page, the
internal ROM before it loads, with ONE register stage on intc8rom ("a second would still be safe,
a third would not"). L1-D never holds a $Cxxx line or a ROM-class line; its $C0xx serves are live
reads of the latch, not lines.

### 3.2 Coherence invariants (each one has a hardware or bench failure behind it) [C]

**3.2.1 Every write that lands in the image snoops every holder by PHYSICAL identity before or
exactly as it reaches the banks.** Two sources (the classified stream cl_* and L1-D's own posted
write ws_*), each with its own compare set OR'd behind the other (rtl/iline.sv:574-586). Retained
(out-of-service) lines are compared too; L1-I KILLS (it has no splice), L1-D SPLICES. The fill in
flight is snooped and restarted (rtl/iline.sv:814-820).

**3.2.2 The store into the very next opcode is one clock from its fetch** (rtl/iline.sv:180-185,
rtl/dline.sv:83-88): the posted-write snoop cannot wait for the drain. A late kill is fenced by a
live 3-bit set match (a write anywhere in the fetch's set costs one clock; rtl/iline.sv:592-626).

**3.2.3 The P+2 law, generalised to the FIFO's depth** (docs/DLINE.md:127-137, 496-509;
page_cache's il_ack refused while pw_pending): a store reaches the banks at P+3, a fill granted
at P+2 read the pre-write byte and nothing restarted it: compiled sprites fetched stale, 1877
stale reads at $3CF5 -> 0 on hardware. "Do NOT assume the single-cycle P+2 fix covers a
multi-entry FIFO; it does not."

**3.2.4 Splicing a clock late is safe because the ISA never reads a data address the clock
after writing it**, and that argument is CHECKED by a sim assertion, never fenced live
(rtl/dline.sv:88-92, 1132-1137): a live fence cost 0.3 ns in the cyc_en cone.

**3.2.5 The dispatcher does not dispatch while a posted write is pending**, and the two write
sources are never both on one clock (rtl/dline.sv:1217-1231). The stream sees the write AT
RETIRE, in program order; write, event, splice and FIFO push are rooted in one edge and nothing is
re-derived later.

**3.2.6 A claim can only be late, never wrong** (rtl/dline.sv:1461-1469): aborted by a splice to
its identity, by any splice on the shadow's install clock, by map_changed; a trigger on a retire
clock is dropped. The battery caught three distinct bugs in the first cut, all "a wrong byte in a
way".

**3.2.7 A page-cache eviction never invalidates an L1 line** (the bytes stay the bank's truth
for that tag; docs/ISTREAM.md:199-201).

### 3.3 Translation and the map: how L1 survives a switch

**3.3.1 No TLB in any serve cone [C].** Serve tags are VIRTUAL; physical identity (ppn) is stored
per line for snoops only. A physically-tagged serve failed at -0.689 ns; a 256-entry TLB on the
fetch clock at -4.8 ns (docs/MEMORY_REFACTOR.md:27-31).

**3.3.2 Region table + per-line revalidation replaces the flash-clear [C]**
(docs/MEMORY_REFACTOR.md rungs 2-3). apple_mmu keeps two 13-entry region tables (read/write;
{sh, cls, wr, bsel, fold, fs}; rtl/apple_mmu.sv:178-213). Each line stores its REGION; on
map_changed the valid bit is recomputed from the stored identity against the new table; the
line's ADDRESS never enters. Unaffected lines stay valid; affected lines keep data and revive.
Motivation: AUXMOVE flips RAMRD/RAMWRT ~51k/s while the caller (LC RAM) and AUXMOVE (ROM) live
in regions those switches never touch.

**3.3.3 What map_changed covers [02]**: every soft switch, the LC, the RamWorks bank register,
INTC8ROM, reset. map_settling is combinational over map_changed and its two successors
(registering it served main's INX where aux's DEX was due; rtl/apple_mmu.sv:1299). A
switch-changing synthetic commit holds turbo_ok five clocks: Nox Archaist hung on hardware from
one byte served from the old bank (rtl/accel_seq.sv:433-460).

**3.3.4 What each reset does [C]**: the card's rst clears everything; inval (the image going
away) clears lines, sets, FIFO; !serve also clears wants and resets the engine; **RES' clears the
predictors and the last byte/row only: lines and the posted FIFO survive, because RAM survives a
reset** (rtl/iline.sv:98-104, rtl/dline.sv:1106-1107).

**3.3.5 The MMU query ports [C]**: L1-I asks for its page and gets p_ppn/p_reg/p_fold/p_fs
(servable, carrying the $C3 rule); L1-D asks from the REGISTERED candidate so the cone runs
through the serve cycle, never on d_pred's late cone.

### 3.4 The page-cache line port: L1's one miss path [C]

One shared port, L1-I first always, arbitrated by hand in master_top (2130-2140). Request =
physical page + line-in-page; row = 16 bytes; **il_done exactly 3 clocks after il_ack**, with
il_ok; a non-resident page DECLINES (the op rides the dispatcher and its miss fills the page).
il_ack is the coherence gate (rtl/page_cache.sv:317-318). op_row: the dispatcher's own read hands
back its whole row so a data miss costs one page-cache access, not two. Install is deliberately
NOT gated by the settle (40-70 ps on a CE; rtl/iline.sv:889-902).

### 3.6 Timing structure and the house laws

The named chains and their cuts (for the record): iscan S_TGT retime (four arithmetic events in
one beat -> the scanner grants itself a beat), the 4-bit line-offset walk with shadows registered

**The house laws a redesign inherits as requirements [C]:**
- Late verdicts pick, they never compute (rtl/iline.sv:409-413).
- No serve cone chains two arithmetic events (docs/DLINE.md:278-280).
- The serve verdict is a registered compare against registered state; never serve in the
  address's own clock (docs/DLINE.md:265-267).
- Reset is control state only, never a live gate in a serve cone (docs/DLINE.md:275-276).
- One explicit write port per LUTRAM or the array flop-izes (4 Kb became 4 k flops, 27.8k of
  20.8k LUTs on the Au; rtl/dline.sv:328-332).
- Nothing below a generate block may be named inside it (17 undriven nets, every posted write in
  page zero on the card; rtl/master_top.sv:654-665; tools/check_generate_scope.py).
- Census counters two flops from any cone (rtl/fast_census.sv:6-15).
- The verdict is taken over more than one build (§1.8).
- When a first hop costs a third of the period and the logic after it a tenth, the answer is not
  a pipeline stage but asking what that one net has to reach (docs/MEMORY_REFACTOR.md:870-920).

## 4. The L2 page cache and the MMU (page_cache, page_client, apple_mmu, fd_bank, fb_sim)

### 4.1 The physical space: a naming contract shared with the host [02 contents, C shape]

PPN = {bank[7:0], page[7:0]}, 16 MB. Bank 0 main, bank 1 the machine's aux (RamWorks bank 0),
RamWorks k at k+1 (254 banks), $FF the ROM at its own offsets (rtl/page_cache.sv:5-15). The host
mirror uses the same layout byte for byte and the same classification rules line for line (host
src/apple_memory.hpp:38-58): a disagreement is a bug, not two opinions.

Hard rules: the LC fold stays inside a bank (LC bank 1's $D000 lives at that bank's $C000-$CFFF);
$Cxxx CPU addresses never translate to a RAM bank's $Cx pages; $C000-$C0FF is BUS always.

### 4.2 The cache's service contract [C unless marked]

**4.2.1 Three clocks from op_present to the byte for every byte of a resident page** (T_FAST=3,
sim/page_cache_tb.sv:43, 567-574). P0 indexes every way and the directory from the PREVIEW ppn
(available a clock before classification), P1 eight tag compares -> one-hot way (a write's
decision here), P2 row mux + byte select and the write LANDS, P3 sub_en/sub_data/decided. One
entrant per clock, three kinds on this card (op, posted write, line request). No tag bypass: one
op at a time.

**4.2.2 A line request's 16-byte row stands exactly 3 clocks after il_ack with il_ok**
(rtl/page_cache.sv:490, combinational off the ack shift register: registering it cost a clock). A
non-resident page declines; the op rides the dispatcher and its miss fills the page.

**4.2.3 op_row: a dispatcher read served from a resident page hands back its whole row** beside
the byte (rtl/page_cache.sv:128-135), never from a fill's critical beat.

**4.2.4 Geometry [02]**: today 128 sets x 8 ways of 256-byte pages = 256 KB, hashed set index
ppn[6:0] ^ ppn[13:7], the whole IIe (128 KB + ROM = 576 pages) in five of every set's eight ways
so an ordinary IIe never evicts (proven: 569 fills, 0 flushes on the Nox map). Directory 18
bits/way/set in LUTRAM {valid, dirty, tag16}; tree-PLRU; invalid way first. Rows in way_ram, true
dual port, 16 byte lanes, 8 RAMB36 per way. **On this card the geometry is bounded by §0**: 64x8
(32 RAMB36) is proven in the bench and is the LARGEST candidate, not the answer: §0 asks for a
significant scale-back, and the never-evict property becomes a design question.

**4.2.5 Write-back, one dirty bit per page, a dirty page flushes WHOLE** (docs/PAGE_CACHE.md:193-197).
Every miss fills (no claim-in-place). Nothing ever clears the image: a cold machine reads DDR3
power-up garbage like a real Apple; the clear walk was built and torn out.

**4.2.6 The miss engine**: E_SNAP 17 clocks (dirty victim only), E_FILL 16 pipelined beats
critical-line-first (the byte served the moment its beat lands, retag at the last), E_PATCH (write
miss), E_SETTLE, E_FLUSH 16 beats. Clean-victim fill ~30 clocks op_present to byte (gate <= 45);
DDR3 critical line mean 11 clocks, max 16; whole fill+flush max 53; on the 8 Gb model 45/56 with a
refresh inside, transaction 83/99 (docs/PAGE_CACHE.md:19-29, 244-253). Hit-under-miss is required
and free. A decline is legal mid-fill.

**4.2.7 The 1 MHz deadline**: op at phi0-fall+2, byte latched at rise+44: ~91 clocks of budget.
One miss at a time: a miss while the engine is busy is counted (late_fills) and NOT served, which
for a write loses the byte. Only the ROM loader may start a fill asynchronously, window-gated
(rtl/rom_loader.sv:31-47). late_fills must read zero.

**4.2.8 Refresh is UberDDR3's own timer; the manual gate is gone** (2026-09-14): a refresh-hit
transaction's tail is absorbed as a stall, the byte is never late. Under acceleration the
client's window is the whole cycle.

### 4.3 Coherence invariants [C]

**4.3.1 The P+2 law** (rtl/page_cache.sv:317-318): il_ack = il_req && !op_present && !pw_take
&& !wl_v && !il_busy && !pw_pending && !iv_run && state != E_PATCH. A fill may not read a page on
the clock a write lands, nor while a posted write is short of the cache, nor during a patch. A
fill acked BEFORE a landing may read the pre-write row because the classified snoop upstream
kills the line. Swept in the bench over k = -4..+4.

**4.3.2 An op never collides with a landing**: busy covers P0..P2; the dispatcher holds on
d_pending. Counted, not assumed: cnt_land_clash and cnt_ppn_clash MUST read zero.

**4.3.3 The dirty bit is forwarded one clock** (rtl/page_cache.sv:396-405): a victim chosen with
a stale dirty bit drops a page's writes.

**4.3.4 Three writers, one order**: snoop writes (the real CPU, and every override: HD DMA, VBL
lock, boot hold are OPS the machine's MMU sees, so the shadow and the image must too), soft-core
writes (retired in L1, posted), and the ROM loader. No host poke exists. The event reaches the
stream at RETIRE, in program order; the merge keeps arrival order and the bus's word wins a
collision. Map changes wait for the FIFO to empty.

**4.3.5 The write tap**: every byte landing in physical bank 0, on the clock it lands, and
nothing else, is on mw_stb (fb_sim's side copy of text + hi-res, read-first collision semantics
wanted). fb_sim's prediction budget is three clocks from a scanner position to its byte. The side
copy was deleted once and REVERSED (2026-09-03): under acceleration the shared port gave the
scanner every other clock at best.

**4.3.6 The monitor compares only banks 0 and 1** at op_commit; unwritten memory is unknown, so
the number to watch is whether mismatches PLATEAU.

### 4.4 Translation (apple_mmu)

**4.4.1 One function, xlate(va, rw), asked for every question so they cannot drift [C]**: the op,
the divert, the sequencer's class, L1-I's page, L1-D's page (rtl/apple_mmu.sv:869-1075). A region
table of 13 regions of the 16-bit space, two registered tables (read/write) of {sh, cls, wr, bsel,
fold, fs}, recomputed every clock; xlate is then a mux + fold + bank select. The tables stand ONE
CLOCK BEHIND the switches (the clock map_changed rises): nothing may read them in that clock.

**4.4.2 The IIe switches [02]**: 80STORE, RAMRD, RAMWRT, INTCXROM, ALTZP, SLOTC3ROM, PAGE2,
HIRES, 80COL, ALTCHAR, TEXT, MIXED, the LC's four-state $C08x with the two-consecutive-reads
pre-write rule; INTC8ROM set at op_present of a $C3xx access with SLOTC3ROM clear, cleared at
op_commit of $CFFF or RES'. RES' forces the IIe MMU's initial state and (by decision) clears the
RamWorks bank register. Proven bit-for-bit against the old classify() over 33.3 M checks.

**4.4.3 The shallow preview [C]**: pf_ppn's page and bank bytes come from a three-level cone,
not the end of xlate's nine-LUT cone (the way address was 12 levels / 7.7 ns route), held equal
to xlate by a sim assertion on every op of every bench (rtl/apple_mmu.sv:1340-1417).

**4.4.4 Divert [02]**: a write to a RamWorks bank the machine lacks goes out on the pins as a
READ; the image takes it; the capture records the write with the core's byte. A RamWorks is real
only while serving.

### 4.5 The DDR3 seam and the ROM [C]

- page_client: pipelined Wishbone, 24-bit address, 128-bit data, one address per 16-byte beat,
  beats go out pipelined (16 serialized round trips ~176 clocks would blow the cycle), cyc held
  from first request to last ack whatever the grant does, in-order ack demux by a 32-entry kinds
  ring. A page's 16 lines sit in one DDR3 row. image_ready = calib_complete.
- Bank map: wb_addr[9:7] is the DDR3 bank; image = bank 0; TX ring = bank 7. wb_arb: page_client
  is a strict-priority pre-emptive slot above the ring's round-robin; a fill queues behind at most
  one accepted burst + a turnaround (~8 clocks). docs/DDR3_USAGE.md's conclusion: optimise so the
  Apple's latency-critical access never queues behind anything; throughput is free.
- The ROM is bank $FF (64 pages) written down the posted-write seam by rom_loader (512-byte
  chunks, per-chunk ack with sum16 and a status, an OVERRUN dropped whole so rom_ok stays low,
  REQUIRE_HOLD, chunk 0 restarts). The seam is arbitrated by hand between L1-D and the loader in
  master_top:2082-2085 (three signals: who offers, who is told, who may offer; getting two of
  three right cost 16,384 dropped L1 writes).

### 4.6 Reset and clean start [C]

The cache survives RES'; only the card's rst walks the directory (SETS clocks, long before the
DDR3 calibrates). RES' resets the MMU switches and the RamWorks register and nothing else. RES'
is held until the ROM has landed (start_pending, cleared only by the host's START_RELEASE;
docs/CLEAN_START.md). The reset sequence rides the bus (§2.1.6).

### 4.11 What the host expects [C]

The same layout and classification byte for byte; classification uses the switches as they
stood before the cycle; every write in the stream in program order with its bank byte (diverted
RamWorks writes as writes, HD DMA as overrides, fast writes class 1); SKIP words accounting for
every unsent cycle; a lost event is a lost write and note_gap counts it; peek/memdump read the
HOST's image (there is no card-side image read at all; fbpeek reads fb_sim's copy, which is what
makes it a check of card against mirror); HD writes come out of the mirror; DMA answers spaced
one byte per bus cycle with one buffer and no queue. The host stays one program for both cards,
selected by its machine key.

## 5. What the list says (observations, not design)

1. **The requirements that are truly fixed are few**: §0, §1, §2.1 (the core's cycle contract),
   §2.3 (the oracles), §3.2/§4.3 (coherence), §4.1/§4.11 (the host's layout and stream), and
   the test obligations. Everything in §2.2, §3.1, §3.3-3.7, §4.2, §4.4-4.9 is the current
   SOLUTION and its scars.
2. **Three serve paths at three latencies is the shape the histogram indicts**: L1 1 clk, the
   dispatcher 7.7 clk, the bus 98 clk, with the dispatcher's 7.6% of ops costing 36% of clocks.
   Every rung since 2026-08-31 moved ops from the middle path to the fast one; the middle path's
   per-op price never moved.
3. **L1 is duplicating the L2's job**: two set-associative caches (8x4 + 8x4 + a retained set)
   with their own revalidation, snoops, fill FSMs and books, in front of a 256 KB cache that
   answers in 3 clocks and never evicts on the IIe. On this card the Au budget makes that
   duplication unaffordable: 12.7k LUTs and 64 RAMB36 for L1 + L2 today, against ~12k and ~45.
4. **The serve-cone laws are the real timing requirements** (§3.6). They are stated eleven
   different ways across three modules today.
5. **The map is restated five times in apple_mmu and once more in each L1 side**; the per-line
   region index is the one representation that worked. A redesign chooses ONE representation of
   translation and ONE of coherence.
6. **Whether the serve latency should be one clock at the CORE's din or one clock at a register
   the core reads next cycle** is the central question this list poses and does not answer.
7. **Carried forward as targets, not requirements**: LC-class writes in L1 (9% of clocks on Nox);
   the never-evict property under the Au's block RAM; the IIe build on the Au part as the first
   measurement.
